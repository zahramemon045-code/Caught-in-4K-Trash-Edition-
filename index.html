Six classes, not two

The base split follows India's Solid Waste Management Rules — green for wet, blue for dry, black for domestic hazardous. Metal and e-waste are pulled out on their own because they need a different grip force and a different destination, and a sixth class exists purely to catch everything the robot isn't sure about.

How a pick works

Four stages between seeing an object and dropping it in a bin. The robot refuses rather than guesses at the third one.

Stage 1

See

The arm-mounted camera frames one object. A pile gets separated first — the classifier is only reliable on a single item.

Stage 2

Classify

The image maps to one of six classes with a confidence score. Soiled items drop out of dry into wet; anything with a battery goes straight to hazardous.

Stage 3

Decide

Below 70% confidence, or hazardous at any confidence, the arm stops and signals instead of picking. A wrong bin is worse than no pick.

Stage 4

Place

Grip force and approach come from the class — slow and wide for metal edges, sealed for wet, never crushing for e-waste.
